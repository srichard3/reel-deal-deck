# Getting found — the launch playbook

Written for Ken and Audrey. No jargon, no filler. Do these things in this order.

---

## 0. The problem to solve first

**Nobody can find you.** During research for this site I searched for "Reel Deal Deck fly fishing"
and the brand did not appear anywhere in the results. What did appear:

- Reel Deal Sportfishing (Florida charter, active on four social platforms)
- Reel Deal Fishing Adventures (Columbia River)
- Multiple other "Reel Deal" charter operations
- A Steam video game called "Reel & Deal"

"Reel Deal" is one of the most heavily used puns in the entire fishing industry. This has two
consequences you need to accept before doing anything else:

1. **You will not win a search for "Reel Deal."** Do not spend money or effort trying.
2. **Your traffic has to come from somewhere other than your brand name.** That is exactly what the
   Fly Library on this site is for.

### Is the name defensible?

Honest answer: the name is fine as a *brand*, weak as a *search asset*, and hard to protect legally.
`™` can be used on an unregistered mark today. A federal registration would be an uphill fight given
how crowded "Reel Deal" is in fishing-related classes — if you care about owning it, talk to a
trademark attorney before you spend on packaging. **This is a legal question, not one to guess at.**

What you *can* own is **"The Reel Deal Deck"** as an exact phrase, and the **Fly Library** as a
destination. Be relentlessly consistent:

- One domain: `reeldealdeck.com`
- The same handle everywhere: `@reeldealdeck` — Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, Pinterest
- Claim them all today even if you will not use them all. They are free and they will be taken.

## 1. Week one — the technical foundation

| Task | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Verify the domain | [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) | Without it you are blind. This is the single most important account you will create. |
| Submit `sitemap.xml` | Search Console → Sitemaps | Tells Google all 62 pages exist at once instead of waiting to be crawled |
| Verify on Bing | [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters) | Bing also feeds DuckDuckGo and ChatGPT search. Free, five minutes. |
| Create Google Business Profile | [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) | Eagle, Idaho. Even for an online brand this wins local and brand searches, and gives you a Knowledge Panel. |
| Claim all social handles | — | See above. Today. |

**What to check weekly in Search Console** (ten minutes, every Monday):

1. **Pages → Indexed** — is the number climbing toward 62? If pages sit in "Discovered, not indexed"
   for more than a month, they need internal links or better content.
2. **Performance → Queries** — what are people *actually* searching to find you? This is the most
   valuable free market research you will ever get. Expect fly names, not your brand name.
3. **Performance → Pages** — which fly pages are getting impressions? Those are the ones to expand.

## 2. The Fly Library is the engine

This is the strategy. Everything else supports it.

Each of the 54 fly pages targets searches that real anglers type constantly:

| Page | Targets |
|---|---|
| `/flies/adams/` | "what is an adams fly", "adams dry fly pattern", "what does an adams imitate" |
| `/flies/san-juan-worm/` | "san juan worm pattern", "is the san juan worm a real fly" |
| `/flies/pheasant-tail-nymph/` | "best nymph for trout", "what does a pheasant tail imitate" |
| `/flies/zebra-midge/` | "winter tailwater fly", "best small nymph trout" |

Nobody searches for your brand. Everybody searches for the flies. The library gets you in front of
anglers who have never heard of you, at the exact moment they are curious about fly patterns — and
then shows them a deck that puts all 54 in their hand.

**Why the library is free and ungated.** No email wall, no sign-up. A gate would kill the rankings,
the links and the goodwill, and it would earn you maybe a few hundred addresses. Being genuinely
useful in public is the whole business case.

### 90-day content cadence

- **Days 1–30:** Do not add pages. Let the 54 get indexed. Fix anything Search Console complains about.
- **Days 31–60:** Add 3–4 "cluster" articles that link *into* the fly pages. These target broader
  searches the individual pages cannot: *"What flies do I actually need to start fly fishing?"*,
  *"Dry fly vs nymph vs streamer, explained"*, *"A first fly box: 12 patterns that cover most water"*,
  *"What to fish in winter."* Each one should link to 8–15 fly pages.
- **Days 61–90:** Look at Search Console queries. Expand the 5 fly pages already getting impressions.
  Depth on a page that is already ranking beats a new page from zero, every time.

### The social calendar writes itself

**One card a day for 54 days.** Photograph the card, post the fly, link the library page.
That is a complete content calendar with zero creative overhead, and it maps 1:1 to pages that
already exist on this site.

This directly fixes the biggest execution gap in the Kickstarter: **it has been running for two
weeks with zero updates posted.** Update cadence drives both backer trust and Kickstarter's own
discovery algorithm. Start posting today, then every 4–5 days minimum.

Audrey's 100k+ views of social experience is the single most valuable asset this brand has and it is
currently invisible — there is no findable brand account. That is the highest-return fix available.

## 3. Where the audience actually is

**Kickstarter's "Playing Cards" category is the wrong room.** That category is dominated by
cardistry and magic decks judged on design sophistication — a contest this deck will not win, against
an audience that does not fish. Meanwhile the people who *want* this product are not browsing
Kickstarter categories at all.

They are here:

- **r/flyfishing** and r/flytying — hundreds of thousands of members
- **Fly-fishing Instagram and YouTube** — guides, shops, and personalities with engaged niches
- **Trout Unlimited chapters** — roughly 400 of them, each with a newsletter and events
- **Local fly shops** — the counter is where impulse gifts get sold
- **Guide services and lodges** — client gifts and tips
- **Fly-fishing forums** — smaller, older, and disproportionately made of serious buyers

## 4. Earning links (the legitimate kind)

Links from respected sites are still how search engines decide who to trust. Every one of these is
earned by being useful or by being a real business, not by buying anything:

1. **Trout Unlimited** — you are already a TU Business member. Get listed in the business directory,
   and offer chapters a fundraising angle (a percentage of deck sales at a chapter banquet).
2. **Fly shops that stock you** — most have a "brands we carry" page. Ask for the link when you close
   the wholesale deal. It costs them nothing.
3. **Gift roundups** — outdoor and gift publications build "best gifts for anglers" lists every
   autumn. Pitch in **August/September**, not December. They write early.
4. **Local Idaho press** — a fifth-generation Idaho farming family and their daughter launching a
   product is a genuinely good local story. Boise outlets, Idaho Statesman, regional lifestyle press.
5. **The LMU angle** — Loyola Marymount's College of Business Administration promotes alumni
   ventures. Alumni magazines and entrepreneurship program features are real, easy links.
6. **Fly-fishing podcasts** — there are dozens, most need guests, and the origin story is good radio.
7. **Guides and instructors** — a teaching tool for clients is a natural fit. Give some away.

## 5. What NOT to do

These will actively damage the brand. Every one of them is a common temptation.

- **Do not use AI to mass-produce fly content.** Your entire credibility rests on experienced anglers
  respecting what the library says. One wrong attribution or one obviously generated paragraph and
  you lose the audience that matters most. Ken should read every fly page before launch.
- **Do not keyword-stuff.** "Fly fishing playing cards fly fishing cards fly fishing gift" reads as
  spam to both Google and humans.
- **Do not buy links or pay for guest posts.** It is against Google's guidelines and it is detectable.
- **Do not claim nobody makes fly-fishing flashcards.** It is false and checkable — Inkstone ($9.95),
  Go Fly Fish ($11.95), RIO ($15.99) and River's Edge all exist, and Orvis publishes free flash cards.
  A knowledgeable angler who catches you in that claim will not buy anything from you. Win on
  original hand-drawn art, real Bicycle stock, and conservation instead. All three are true.
- **Do not fake urgency.** "Only 3 left!" on a product that has not been printed destroys trust.
- **Do not gate reviews.** Asking only happy customers for reviews violates FTC guidance.
- **Do not run ads yet.** With no product to ship and no email list, paid traffic is money set on
  fire. Revisit once you have inventory and a converting page.
