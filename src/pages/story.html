Hand-drawn playing cards that teach while you play

That is their line, not ours, and it is the clearest description of this product anyone has written. A deck of cards gets dealt, argued over and left on a kitchen table. Fifty-four flies on fifty-four cards get looked at hundreds of times without anybody deciding to study anything. That is the entire idea.

Ken went looking for a set of fly-fishing flashcards he would actually want to own. He found several. He didn’t want any of them — so he drew his own, one pattern at a time, until there were enough for a deck. Every illustration on every card is original and drawn by hand.

Turning drawings into a deck meant one decision the whole product rests on: print it on real playing card stock. Not board with rounded corners — {{ site.product.stock }}, at {{ site.product.dimensions }}, from {{ site.product.manufacturer }}. It costs more per deck. It is also the difference between a reference card and a deck of cards, and a deck of cards gets played with.

Two people, related to each other

Audrey is Ken’s daughter and an entrepreneurship graduate of Loyola Marymount University’s College of Business Administration. She handles design direction, brand and community — the polite way of saying she is the reason the drawings became a product rather than a folder on a laptop.

They are farmers and anglers before they are a company. The deck is made in {{ site.location.city }}, {{ site.location.regionName }}, and answered by the two of them personally.
