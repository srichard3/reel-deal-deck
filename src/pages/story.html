Eagle, Idaho

He couldn't find the deck he wanted, so he drew it.

The Reel Deal Deck is made by two people who are related to each other: Ken, who draws the flies, and Audrey, his daughter, who makes them into something you can hold.

Ken is a fifth-generation Idaho farmer and a lifelong entrepreneur, which in practice means he has spent his whole life around two things: land that only pays attention to the people who pay attention to it, and businesses started from scratch. He came to fly fishing as an amateur and stayed one on purpose — the good kind of amateur, the kind who wants to know why a pattern works and not just that it does.

The problem was learning the flies. There are thousands of patterns and the ones that matter are separated from the ones that don't by experience nobody hands you. He went looking for a set of reference cards he could keep in a vest pocket or leave on the kitchen table. He found several. He didn't want to own any of them — they were printed on stock that curled, illustrated with borrowed photographs, and built like a giveaway rather than a thing.

So he started drawing

One fly at a time, in the evenings. A hook, a body, the hackle, the profile the fish actually sees. Drawing a fly turns out to be the fastest way to learn it: you cannot draw a Pheasant Tail without deciding, precisely, where the thorax ends and the wing case begins. The drawings were for himself. Then there were forty of them. Then there were enough for a deck.

Turning that into a deck instead of a stack of drawings meant one non-negotiable decision, and it is the decision the whole product rests on: print it on real playing card stock. Not 300gsm board with rounded corners — genuine Bicycle stock from USPCC, with the Air-Cushion finish, the same card people have been shuffling for a century and a half. It costs more per deck. It is also the difference between a reference card and a deck of cards, and a deck of cards gets played with, which means it gets looked at, which means the flies get learned.

Audrey's half

Audrey is Ken's daughter and an entrepreneurship graduate of Loyola Marymount University's College of Business Administration. She handles design direction, brand and community — which is the polite way of saying she is the reason the drawings became a product rather than a folder on a laptop.

The two of them argue about typography and hook proportions, mostly by text message, occasionally in person. There is no third co-founder, no design agency and no stock-photo budget. Every illustration on every card is original and drawn by hand. That is not a marketing line; it is just the only way this deck could have been made.

What we are not going to tell you

We are not going to tell you nobody else makes fly-fishing cards. Several companies do, some of them good, and one search would embarrass us. What we will claim is narrower and true: the art is ours and hand-drawn, the stock is genuine Bicycle, and a set amount from every deck goes to Trout Unlimited. That combination didn't exist, which is the only reason to build another one.
