## The origin, in their telling

> Fly fishing is full of beauty, tradition, and endless learning.

> If you've spent any time around the sport, you know there are hundreds of different
> flies, each with its own name, purpose, history, and style. That can feel exciting but
> also overwhelming, even for the most experienced anglers!

> That is where this project began.

> Our fly fishing playing cards started with one simple question: Why isn't there an
> easy, enjoyable way to learn the names and varieties of flies?

> Ken… is also a cool, avid amateur fly fisherman who genuinely wanted to understand the
> sport better and fit in more comfortably with the friends and fellow anglers he spent
> time with, who loved talking about flies, patterns, and techniques. While he's
> comfortable on the water, some of the finer details were a little over his head.

> He couldn't even find a set of fly fishing flashcards. So he thought, why not make his
> own? And then came the twist: Why not make them double as playing cards?

**Careful here.** Their line is "he couldn't even find a set." The site must not turn
that into "nobody makes fly-fishing flashcards" — several companies do, and one search
disproves it (see `README.md` → Positioning). Write it as *his* experience of looking,
never as a claim about the market.

> What started as a simple learning tool turned into something much more fun, beautiful,
> and giftable.

> These cards have already helped Ken become a more knowledgeable angler, and now we hope
> they can do the same for others — whether you are just getting into fly fishing, have
> been at it for years, or simply love the art and culture that surround the sport.

## How to write in this voice

The campaign is warm, plain-spoken and a little enthusiastic. It says "we", names Ken and
Audrey, admits what it does not know, and is unembarrassed about hoping you like the thing.

**Do**

- Say *we*, and say *Ken* and *Audrey* by name.
- Let a sentence be glad. "It made us grin" is on-brand; so is "Tight lines."
- Keep the honesty. They volunteer that Ken was out of his depth; that candour is the
  warmth, not a flaw to edit out.
- Keep sentences short and concrete. Real nouns: a kitchen table, a cabin, a gear bag.

**Don't**

- Perform detachment. Earlier drafts of this site used lines like "What we are not going
  to tell you" — accurate, but colder than the family it describes.
- Reach for superlatives they never used, or invent a riverbank scene. The guardrail in
  `CLAUDE.md` still holds: never invent a fact, however charming.
- Let warmth push the answer-first block down a fly page or a guide. Those open with the
  answer; the voice lives in the prose around it.
